Purpose & context

Lane is conducting a deep, scholarly unit-by-unit study of the Gospel of Matthew, collaborating with a study partner who works from English only. The project's goal is to produce integrated literary and theological study materials for each of 28 literary units, combining prose commentary with structured HTML translation artifacts. Lane is heavily influenced by the Bible Project's literary-canonical reading approach, and the project holds that framework in active dialogue with Constable's dispensationalist commentary throughout — explicitly flagging where the dispensationalist frame strains against France, Wright, and Davies-Allison.

Lane has only a small amount of Greek and Hebrew knowledge. All Greek and Hebrew must be carried by the English at all times: always provide native script first with transliteration in parentheses (e.g., δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosynē), תָּמִים (tamim)), always gloss the meaning in plain English, never assume a term is "already known" from earlier in the same document or a prior unit, and never let an argument depend on Lane parsing script, morphology, or grammatical terminology unaided. Explain grammatical categories (aorist, dative, participle, etc.) in plain terms whenever they carry weight. This applies everywhere: chat commentary, pre-read briefings, and HTML artifacts (verse text, glosses, compare boxes, endnotes, legends). Any earlier project notes claiming intermediate Greek literacy are incorrect.

Key learnings & principles

Interpretive tension as a feature: Constable's dispensational readings are consistently held in active dialogue with France/Wright/Davies-Allison rather than one simply displacing the other; the project explicitly names where readings diverge and why.
Cross-unit threads require active curation: The project distinguishes between unit-local observations and threads that warrant formal tracking across the gospel. Lane decides which observations to promote; Claude flags candidates.
Project memory is authoritative over the style reference file when the two diverge on cross-unit thread tracking.
Surgical editing over rewriting: Lane's consistent preference is for targeted edits to project files rather than wholesale rewrites.
Depth over speed: Units are split mid-pass (e.g., verse-by-verse in two halves) when necessary to give full treatment to cruxes rather than moving past them quickly.
Scoping confirmation before building: Claude surfaces open scoping questions and confirms the approach before producing each unit's deliverables.
Discrepancy surfacing: When Lane's request conflicts with prior session records (e.g., requesting a unit already shipped), Claude flags the conflict explicitly with a structured clarifying question rather than silently rebuilding or proceeding.
Constellation of signals for structure: Unit divisions are grounded in converging structural evidence — Kingsbury's ἀπὸ τότε (apo tote) hinges, Bacon's five discourse-closing formulas, the Mackie/Bible Project macro-design, and Davies-Allison's triadic patterns and Emmanuel inclusio.

Approach & patterns

Each unit follows a three-pass structure: (1) flowing-prose pre-read briefing in chat; (2) verse-by-verse commentary in chat (split into halves when depth demands it); (3) standalone HTML study-translation artifact. The pre-read covers literary architecture, historical and intertextual context, key Greek/Hebrew vocabulary to watch, and the major interpretive tensions to hold. The artifact includes color-coded recurring Greek roots (fresh per-unit color assignments, not carried across units), visual structural diagrams (ring maps, correspondence tables, chiasm diagrams, architecture tables), verse-by-verse wooden translation with inline glosses and compare boxes at translation-sensitive verses, and numbered endnotes with hyperlinks to external lexical and textual resources.

Translation philosophy: preserve Greek wordplay and repeated roots that English versions typically obscure; use "y'all" only for genuine second-person plural address in the text; OT roots and forward NT trajectories treated as non-optional at significant terms; LXX freight on Greek words flagged with the underlying Hebrew theological concept.

Research draws on: Greek text (SBLGNT via Matt.txt), NASB (MatthewNASB.txt), Constable's commentary, Bible Project teacher notes, patristic commentary (primarily Chrysostom), and scholarly voices (France, Wright, Davies-Allison), supplemented by Sefaria, Biblehub, and web searches for rabbinic parallels and Second Temple sources.

Live cross-unit threads (close each loop at its payoff unit; add new ones as opened):

Light handoff — φῶς (phōs, "light") dawns on the people at 4:16 (Isa 9) → "y'all are the light of the world" at 5:14 (Unit 5); watch the light/darkness pair across the 4/5 seam; updated in Unit 6 with the possibility of light curdling back to darkness (6:23).
Nations/ἔθνη (ethnē) seed — "Galilee of the nations" 4:15 → "make disciples of all the nations" 28:19 (Unit 28); one leg of the Emmanuel/nations frame bracketing the whole book.
ἀκολουθέω (akoloutheō, "follow") crowd vs. disciple — used of the four (4:20, 22) AND of the crowds (4:25); the ambiguity is what 5:1 opens on; track as a discipleship keyword from Unit 4 on. In Unit 8, ἀκολουθέω (akoloutheō) finally lands cleanly on οἱ μαθηταί (hoi mathētai, "the disciples") at 8:23 after two failed would-be followers.
Fishing/net imagery — "fishers of men" 4:19 carries OT judgment freight (Jer 16:16; Hab 1; Amos 4; Ezek 29) in tension with gathering-for-restoration; resurfaces in the dragnet parable 13:47–50 (Unit 13).
Two ἀπὸ τότε (apo tote, "from that time") hinges — 4:17 (Unit 4, "began to proclaim") ↔ 16:21 (Unit 17, "began to show he must suffer"); read the two together when Unit 17 arrives.
Law & Prophets inclusio — opens 5:17, closes 7:12, spanned Units 5–7; now closed.
ὀλιγόπιστοι (oligopistoi, "little-faiths") — tracked since Unit 6 as a disciple-keyword; applied to the disciples themselves for the first time at 8:26 in Unit 8. Future payoff units: 14, 16.
ἀφίημι (aphiēmi, "release/forgive") root — the fever's release at 8:15 points forward to the paralytic's forgiveness at 9:2 (Unit 9).
Build/rock thread — planted at 7:24 (Unit 7) pointing forward to 16:18 (Unit 16/17).
φρόνιμος/μωρός (phronimos/mōros, "wise/foolish") contrast — planted at 7:24–26 pointing forward to 25:2 (Unit 25).
ἐξουσία (exousia, "authority") thread — planted at 7:29 pointing forward to 28:18 (Unit 28).
βάλλω (ballō, "throw/cast") web — tormented servant (8:6), expelled sons of the kingdom (8:12), Peter's mother-in-law (8:15), and the storm scene; Unit 8 cluster established.
σεισμός (seismos, "earthquake/shaking") — flagged at 8:24 as a forward echo toward the crucifixion and resurrection earthquakes.
