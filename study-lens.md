# Study lens

What the study looks for and how it talks. Read by every research, writing and
checking step.

## Reader

Lane, in English. A little Greek and Hebrew: transliterate + gloss every Greek
or Hebrew word, again at its first use in each unit. Never native script
(prose, notes, fragment, attributes). Grammar that carries weight (aorist,
participle, middle voice) → plain terms; no argument may depend on parsing the
Greek. Merely unusual grammar → footnote, if anywhere.

## Lens, in order of weight

1. Intertextuality: where Israel's Scriptures stand behind a line (quoted,
   alluded to, echoed; usually via the LXX; name the Hebrew behind it where it
   matters), where the line or word returns later in the NT, what the echo does.
2. Narrative structure, keyword tracing, type-scenes, the creation, covenant,
   exile, presence metanarrative, in the Bible Project's manner.
3. Matthew's own markers alongside what you notice: the five discourse
   formulas, the two *apo tote* hinges, the formula quotations.
4. Chiasms only when real and checkable; weigh them hard, they're easy to make up.

Strong suggestions:

- Constable is the baseline reading, in active dialogue with France, Wright,
  Davies & Allison, the Bible Project, Jewish (Second Temple, rabbinic) and
  patristic voices, especially Chrysostom. Name where readings diverge and why.
- Check every scene Mark, Luke or John tell. Box the ones where Matthew's
  change is telling (addition, omission, reordering, rewording that shifts the
  point): roughly three to six a unit.
- Plausible but thin link, or scholars skeptical → keep it, marked tentative
  with why. Drop only what the text doesn't bear out.
- Fine-grained grammar and any one tradition stay in proportion.

Things to avoid:

- Quotation marks around wording you didn't read in this unit's work
  (status VERIFIED). Otherwise paraphrase.
- Recalling ids, rulings or renderings: copy them from the pack.
- Long quotes from copyrighted commentaries (D&A, France, Barclay): paraphrase.
- Going deep into the Sermon on the Mount or Olivet without flagging it as a
  rabbit hole in the calls.

## Sources

Per unit, already cut: `sources/units/NN/` (Constable, Chrysostom, Davies &
Allison vol. 3 for 19-28; each file opens with the sections it holds). Whole
texts: `sources/text/`, `sources/raw/`. Greek: `MatthewSBLGNT.txt`,
`Matthew-words.tsv`; English comparison: `MatthewWEB.txt`,
`sources/raw/MatthewNASB.txt`. LXX: `python -m biblecore lxx`. Web: search
freely and cite what you read; `resources.md` lists tested sites (Catena Aurea,
Chrysostom, Ryle, Edersheim, Ellicott, BibleProject). Bible Project teacher
notes cover Matthew 1-7 only.

D&A is OCR: Greek and Hebrew in it come out garbled; read the argument, not
the scanned Greek.

## Prose for the ear (briefing, verse by verse)

These are read aloud by the web app's player.

- Flowing prose. A short heading per scene is fine; no bullets, bold, tables,
  verse numbers or references in the text.
- Names as a person says them ("the book of Genesis", not "Gen 40:20").
- Briefing (30,000-foot view): placement in the book, background, genre,
  vocabulary to watch, tensions to hold, earlier stories the unit leans on
  (named, not told). Orient, don't resolve. About 600-900 words.
- Verse by verse: depth over speed. Earlier-Scripture roots and canonical
  trajectories first; then wordplay, structures only where real, background,
  commentary dialogue. Where a verse reaches an earlier story, tell it there:
  background, what matches, what differs, what Matthew does with it. A telling
  difference from a parallel account is said in place. Leave tensions open.
  Devotional weight lightly.
