# Unit 20 notes: Matthew 20:1-34, The Last Shall Be First

Read 19:16-20:16 as one arc (map: watch 19:30 and 20:16). Four scenes: the vineyard parable (1-16), third passion prediction (17-19), Zebedee's family and the Ten (20-28), two blind men at Jericho (29-34). Pack: no recorded LXX phrase matches (0); 13 rare words; 21 tracked threads, none opens here. Pack flags five thread-opener mismatches (father, hand-over, mercy, evil, compassion): reported, not resolved.

## Scene 1: the master of the house and the labourers (20:1-16)

**People, places, objects.** A master of the house (oikodespotēs, 20:1, 11; "lord" kyrios of the vineyard at 20:8), labourers (ergatai, "workers"), a steward (epitropos, 20:8, only here in Matthew; pack lists it as rare), the marketplace (agora, 20:3), a denarius (dēnarion, a day's wage), the vineyard (ampelōn), "the burden of the day and the scorching heat" (baros, kausōn). Vineyard: earlier in Matthew only as a general image; this opens the vineyard run (21:28-32, 21:33-41 build on it; pack: ampelōn only units 20, 21). Master of the house (oikodespotēs): 10:25 (Jesus himself called one), 13:27, 13:52, 21:33 later; here God's role. The mood word "wage" (misthos): 5:12, 6:1ff, 10:41-42, 19:27ff set up the reward question; this parable answers Peter's "what then will we have" (19:27).

**Story shape.** Employer goes out repeatedly to hire; the day ends with payment; complaint; reply. Nearest same-shape story: the elder brother (Luke 15:25-32, read via the synoptic file for Luke only as a parallel in outline; Constable, France and D&A all draw it; D&A gives a structure table). Same: an unearned generosity to the late, a resentful earlier worker, a speech of justification. Different: the elder son is family and the father pleads; here a hired hand is dismissed with "take what is yours and go" and the rhetorical "Is your eye evil because I am good?" Does: pulls the reader into the grumbler's seat.

**Matthew vs parallels.** No synoptic parallel. 20:16 inverts the order of 19:30 (Mark 10:31 has "first last / last first"); Luke 13:30 has "last first / first last", the same order as 20:16 (D&A, read). The long hiring sequence is told with shrinking detail (third, sixth, ninth, eleventh) and only first and last matter in the payoff (France, D&A, Constable agree).

**Scene voices.** Constable: grace, not Israel/Gentile allegory; first-hired = disciples/Jews is one view among several; rewards vary elsewhere (25:14-30), no ranking here. France: equal pay for unequal work; day labourers are involuntary marginals; the man hires out of compassion; do not seek a group to match. D&A: "all attempts to make it true to life have failed"; eight-plus allegorical readings, the dominant history of interpretation is salvation-history (Irenaeus, Origen, Gregory); D&A choose a warning to the faithful (19:30, "do not presume") over a pure grace reading, noting the first do not actually become last in the story. Chrysostom (Hom. 64.3-4): the parable is for late converts and a warning to the proud; "do not press every detail of a parable"; the "first last" is added from outside the parable. Catena: Gregory's five ages of the world; Origen "denarius = salvation"; Jerome "denarius = the image of the king." Ellicott: first-called = the disciples; the young ruler glanced at 20:16. Where they split: reversal (Chrysostom, D&A) vs equality (France, Constable, Pseudo-Chrysostom); I keep both and say the parable itself shows equality while the framing sayings show reversal.

## Scene 2: the third passion prediction (20:17-19)

**People, places, objects.** Jesus, the twelve, Jerusalem (first named as destination since 16:21), chief priests and scribes (archiereis, grammateis), the nations / Gentiles (ethnē), mocking, scourging, crucifixion, "the third day". Earlier: first prediction 16:21 (northern Galilee, "from then" hinge), second 17:22-23 (Galilee; "handed over into the hands of people"). Chief priests and scribes first appear together in 2:4; D&A: "the end will be like the beginning."

**Shape.** Private instruction on the road, three predictions (16:21; 17:22-23; 20:18-19), each followed by a failure of understanding: Peter's rebuke (16:22), the disciples' grief and the question about the greatest (17:23, 18:1), here the Zebedee request (20:20). Nearest same-shape story: the other two predictions; read target: 16:21-23 and 17:22-23 (pack/Greek only, not re-opened here beyond D&A's account).

**Matthew vs parallels.** Mark 10:32-34 and Luke 18:31-34 (both read via synoptic file). Matthew: shorter than Mark (18 words vs 32), drops Mark's amazement and fear of the followers, adds "disciples", "privately". Matthew "they will crucify" for Mark "they will kill"; "on the third day" for Mark "after three days" (D&A, confirmed against Mark's wording in the synoptic file). Luke alone: "all that is written through the prophets" and "they understood none of these things." Mark and Luke have spitting; Matthew drops it, leaving mock-scourge-crucify.

## Scene 3: the mother, the sons and the Ten (20:20-28)

**People, places, objects.** The mother of the sons of Zebedee (unnamed here; Salome by Mark 15:40/16:1 via the Fathers and Ellicott; Matt 27:56 names her only as "the mother of the sons of Zebedee"), James and John (named earlier in 4:21, 10:2, 17:1), "the Ten", the rulers of the nations, the cup (potērion), right hand and left hand ("euōnymos" = "left", lit. "well-named", a euphemism), the Son of Man, a ransom (lytron). Earlier: Zebedee's sons called 4:21-22; transfiguration 17:1; "twelve thrones" 19:28 (the fuel for the request). The mother: another mother kneels and asks in this Gospel, the Canaanite woman (15:21-28, both proskyneō, same verb); Bathsheba before David in 1 Kgs 1.

**Shape.** Petition scene: a suppliant bows, king asks "What do you want?", petition, answer. Nearest earlier same-shape story: Bathsheba/Adonijah (1 Kgs 1:15-21, read in LXX and WEB) and, in Matthew, the Canaanite woman (15:22-25). Same: a mother, a son's succession/throne, bowing (proskyneō), a king's "What do you want?" (Esther 5:3 LXX per D&A). Different: Bathsheba's request is granted and the throne goes to her son; here the request is refused and what sits at right and left in Jerusalem turns out to be two crucified bandits (27:38).

**Matthew vs parallels.** Mark 10:35-45 (read). Matthew: mother speaks, not the sons; adds her bowing; no "do for us whatever we ask"; "command" (eipe) for Mark's "grant" (dos); "in your kingdom" for Mark's "in your glory"; drops the baptism clause from Jesus' question and answer (so Matthew keeps only the cup); adds "of my Father" at 20:23; "the ten were indignant about the two brothers" for Mark's "James and John". Luke has no counterpart to the request; Luke 22:24-27 (read via Luke 18 only, not opened) has the dispute at the Supper, per D&A. Luke 22:24-27 opened in fact-check (WEB): dispute over who is greatest at the Supper, confirmed.

**Scene voices.** Constable: they thought of an earthly kingdom soon; cup = judgment; ransom is the first explanation of why he dies; on "for many" prefers the Servant of Isa 53. France: Matthew is blunt about Peter elsewhere, so the mother is not a cover; wording "kingdom" for "glory"; right-and-left echo of the cross; "Servant (diakonos) vs slave (doulos)"; ransom: primary sense deliverance; echo of Isa 53 is of idea more than Greek words; "limited atonement is anachronistic". D&A: cup = eschatological sorrow poured on God's people first; John's martyrdom disputed; v. 28 combines Dan 7 and Isa 53; Isa 43:3-4 possible; links 1 Kgs 1 and 15:21-28 as explanations for the mother. Chrysostom (Hom. 65): the sons shamed themselves by sending their mother; the right-hand throne belongs to no creature ("not mine to give" is condescension to their understanding); "how low you go, you cannot go lower than your Lord." Catena: Jerome, the names of those seated are withheld so none is shut out; Origen, "he does not say you are able, he says you will drink"; Pseudo-Chrysostom, the mother asked for heavenly things. Ellicott: the sons' closeness (Transfiguration, "Sons of Thunder"); "the greatness is the service." Where they split: whether "not mine to give" means a real ranking prepared (France, D&A) or condescension (Chrysostom); whether the cup includes expiation (Constable) or only suffering (France, D&A).

## Scene 4: the two blind men at Jericho (20:29-34)

**People, places, objects.** The crowd, two blind men (typhloi), the road (hodos), Jericho (Ierichō, pack: only here in Matthew; Heb 11:30 later), "Son of David" (huios Dauid, thread `son-of-david`), mercy (eleaō, thread `mercy`/loyal-love), eyes (ophthalmoi), "gut-wrenched" (splanchnizomai, thread `compassion`, 20:34), "followed" (akoloutheō, 20:29, 34; thread `follow`). Earlier: 9:27-31 (two blind men, "Have mercy on us, Son of David", touch, eyes opened, "followed", with a gag order) and 15:22 (Canaanite woman, same cry); 12:23 (crowds ask "Is this the Son of David?"). Last healing story in Matthew's journey; no secrecy order.

**Shape.** Healing story with obstructing crowd: persistent cry, a rebuke, Jesus stops and asks "What do you want?", the request, touch, sight, following. Nearest earlier same-shape story: 9:27-31 (the same two-blind-men healing) and 19:13-15 (disciples rebuke the children, Jesus welcomes: same verb epitimaō "rebuke" in the pack thread `charge-sharply`). Same: two blind men, the title, the touch of the eyes, the verb "followed". Different: location (Galilee house vs open road near Jericho), no gag order, a crowd that rebukes, "gut-wrenched", Jesus asks the same question he asked James and John (20:21 vs 20:32, same words "What do you want?").

**Matthew vs parallels.** Mark 10:46-52 (read), Luke 18:35-43 (read). Mark: one blind man, named Bartimaeus, a beggar, with a cloak thrown off, "Take heart, get up, he calls you", "your faith has saved you"; Mark "Rabbouni." Luke: one man, on approach to Jericho, the crowd leading rebukes him. Matthew: two, unnamed; no "faith has saved you"; Jesus touches the eyes; "gut-wrenched"; adds "Lord" three times. D&A: assimilated to 9:27-31 and perhaps to Mark 8:22-26 (touch); the doubling riddle unsolved (France: "no better explanation"; Constable: two witnesses; D&A: contrast with the two disciples, parallel with 9:27).

**Scene voices.** Constable: believers who "see" while the disciples don't; kingdom delayed; healed men follow. France: two blind men doubled, no better reason; Jericho the last stop before the climb; "following" may be a model disciple, may only be joining the pilgrim crowd; the crowd is a foil as the disciples were at 19:13-15; no gag order now. D&A: structure by inclusio "followed"; contrast with 20:20-28; blind men's request is direct, titled, and needful. Chrysostom (Hom. 66): they were more perceptive than seeing men; Jesus lets the crowd rebuke them to show their persistence. Catena: Jerome, Jesus stops because of the cliffs and pits; Jericho = this world (Origen, Raban); Pseudo-Chrysostom, "Lord" would confess divinity, "Son of David" only humanity (an old Christian reading, worth saying aloud because it shows how readers once used the title; against the Gospel's own use). Ellicott: the cry became the Kyrie; Luke's placement is explained by local inquiry or misinformation.

## Cross-scene links and Lane-level notes

- The unit's frame: 19:30 / 20:16 first-last inclusio; 20:21, 20:32 "What do you want?"; 20:26-27 "whoever wishes" (thelō) against 20:14-15 "I wish" (thelō): God's will vs our wish; the pack lists thelō 6 times in unit. Verified in the Greek.
- 20:17 "going up" and 20:18 "we are going up": first-person plural (D&A, France). "Going up" three times in 20:17-18 in Greek reading (anabainō at 20:17, 20:18).
- Zebedee's sons: Jesus has already given them privilege (17:1); they appear again at Gethsemane (26:37) per France.
