The Architecture of the Genealogy

A frame that runs the line backward, then forward — Jesus is both the goal and the new beginning

v. 1 Jesus→ David→ Abraham
vv. 2–16 Abraham→ David→ Jesus
the names that open v. 1 in reverse become the pillars of the list — A · D · J mirrored as J · D · A
Abraham → David the king14 generations · ascent · Abrahamic covenant
David → the deportation to Babylon14 generations · descent · the monarchy falls
the deportation → the Anointed14 generations · restoration · Messiah

Three sets of fourteen, with David as the numerical signature: in Hebrew דוד (D-V-D) = 4 + 6 + 4 = 14.
Forty-two generations = six sevens; Jesus opens the seventh seven, the sabbath-fullness.

The Genealogy  ·  1:1–17
1Book of the genesis (βίβλος γενέσεως, biblos geneseōs) of Jesus Anointed,2 son of David, son of Abraham.1
The opening phrase is verbatim the Septuagint heading of Genesis 2:4 and 5:1 — "book of the genesis of heaven and earth / of humankind." A new beginning is being announced. David is named before Abraham: the royal claim comes first.3
2Abraham begot Isaac, and Isaac begot Jacob, and Jacob begot Judah and his brothers,
ἐγέννησεν (egennēsen) — the same begetting verb as the Genesis genealogies. "And his brothers" gestures at the twelve tribes; Judah alone carries the scepter (Gen 49:10).
3and Judah begot Perez and Zerah from Tamar, and Perez begot Hezron, and Hezron begot Aram,
First rupture of the male rhythm: ἐκ τῆς Θαμάρ — the Canaanite who, judged "more righteous" than Judah, secured the line (Gen 38).4
4and Aram begot Amminadab, and Amminadab begot Nahshon, and Nahshon begot Salmon,
5and Salmon begot Boaz from Rahab, and Boaz begot Obed from Ruth, and Obed begot Jesse,
Two more foreign women in one breath — Rahab the Canaanite of Jericho, Ruth the Moabite. Matthew is partly working from the genealogy that closes the book of Ruth (Ruth 4:18–22).
6and Jesse begot David the king. And David begot Solomon from the [wife] of Uriah,
The summit of the line, and Matthew will not let it be clean: not "Bathsheba" but τῆς τοῦ Οὐρίου, "the [woman] of Uriah" — the murdered Hittite's name kept in the ear, David's sin unhidden at the throne.5
7and Solomon begot Rehoboam, and Rehoboam begot Abijah, and Abijah begot Asaph,
The best-attested text reads Ἀσάφ (Asaph, the psalmist) where the king's name should be Asa.6
8and Asaph begot Jehoshaphat, and Jehoshaphat begot Joram, and Joram begot Uzziah,
Three kings are silently omitted between Joram and Uzziah (Ahaziah, Joash, Amaziah) — the count of fourteen is built, not transcribed.
9and Uzziah begot Jotham, and Jotham begot Ahaz, and Ahaz begot Hezekiah,
10and Hezekiah begot Manasseh, and Manasseh begot Amos, and Amos begot Josiah,
Again the surprising form: Ἀμώς (Amos, the prophet) for the king Amon — worship and prophecy folded into the royal names.6
11and Josiah begot Jeconiah and his brothers, at the deportation of Babylon.
μετοικεσία (metoikesia), "deportation," tolls three times across vv. 11–12, 17. "And his brothers" echoes Judah "and his brothers" (v. 2): the monarchy framed between brotherhood at its dawn and at its eclipse.7
12And after the deportation of Babylon, Jeconiah begot Shealtiel, and Shealtiel begot Zerubbabel,
13and Zerubbabel begot Abiud, and Abiud begot Eliakim, and Eliakim begot Azor,
14and Azor begot Zadok, and Zadok begot Achim, and Achim begot Eliud,
15and Eliud begot Eleazar, and Eleazar begot Matthan, and Matthan begot Jacob,
Jacob begetting a Joseph — the patriarchal pairing of Genesis, quietly cueing Joseph the dreamer who is about to appear.
16and Jacob begot Joseph the husband of Mary, from whom was begotten Jesus, the one called Anointed.
The hinge of the chapter. After 39 active "X begot Y" clauses, the verb flips to the passive ἐγεννήθη ("was begotten") and the relative pronoun ἐξ ἧς is feminine singular — "from her," Mary alone. The chain of fathers breaks; a divine agent stands behind the passive.8
NASB "…Joseph the husband of Mary, by whom Jesus was born, who is called the Messiah."
note "by whom Jesus was born" loses the passive's force; the wooden "from whom was begotten" preserves the deliberate seam.
17So all the generations from Abraham until David [are] fourteen generations, and from David until the deportation of Babylon fourteen generations, and from the deportation of Babylon until the Anointed fourteen generations.
Matthew tells you he has counted — the flag that the number is symbolic. Fourteen = the numerical value of David's name; see the architecture panel above.9
The Naming of Jesus  ·  1:18–25
18Now of Jesus Anointed the genesis was thus. His mother Mary having been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together she was found having [a child] in [the] womb from [the] Holy Spirit.
γένεσις returns from v. 1, binding the two halves of the chapter into one "genesis." Betrothal (μνηστευθείσης) was already a binding bond, dissolved only by divorce. Matthew tells the reader the cause before any character knows it.10
19And Joseph her husband, being righteous and not willing to expose her, resolved to release her secretly.
δίκαιος (dikaios) — is he righteous yet merciful, or righteous in being merciful? The verb δειγματίσαι is "to make a public example of." Joseph already embodies the surpassing righteousness of 5:20.11
NASB "being a righteous man and not wanting to disgrace her, planned to send her away secretly."
20But as he was pondering these things — behold, an angel of [the] Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying: Joseph, son of David, do not fear to take alongside Mary your wife, for the [thing] begotten in her is from [the] Holy Spirit;
ἄγγελος κυρίου = the LXX's mal'akh YHWH, the figure at Israel's hinges (Hagar, Moriah, the bush). κατ' ὄναρ, the first of five dreams in the prologue — Joseph the dreamer reborn. He alone besides Jesus is called "son of David."8
21and she will bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus, for he himself will save his people from their sins.
The pun lives only in the Hebrew: Ἰησοῦς = Yēšûaʿ, "YHWH saves" — so "save" (σώσει) glosses the very name. The line all but quotes Psalm 130:8 LXX: "he himself will redeem Israel from all its iniquities."12
22Now this whole [thing] has happened so that what was spoken by [the] Lord through the prophet might be filled full, saying:
The first formula quotation. The Lord is the speaker, the prophet the channel. πληρόω, "fill full" — for Matthew, fulfillment is a story brought to its fullness, not a prediction merely ticked off.
23"Behold, the virgin will have [a child] in [the] womb and will bear a son, and they will call his name Emmanuel" — which is, being translated, "With us [is] God."
Isaiah 7:14. Matthew quotes the Greek παρθένος (parthenos) where the Hebrew has almah, "young woman." He stops to translate the name, because μεθ' ἡμῶν ὁ θεός is the gospel's thesis — and its frame: it returns at 18:20 and closes the book at 28:20, "I am with y'all always."13
NASB "Behold, the virgin shall be with child and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name Immanuel."
Hart leaves "Emmanuel" untranslated in the line and lets Matthew's own gloss "God is with us" carry it — keeping the name, and its meaning, as the point.
Heb / LXX almah (young woman) → παρθένος (virgin): the choice was the Septuagint's, two centuries pre-Christian. NETS Isaiah.
24And Joseph, having been raised from sleep, did as the angel of [the] Lord ordered him, and took alongside his wife,
No recorded word — the silent, obedient man who "hears and does," the very thing Jesus will praise at the Sermon's close (7:24). παρέλαβεν ("took alongside") will recur through the Egypt journeys.
25and he was not knowing her until she bore a son; and he called his name Jesus.
ἐγίνωσκεν, the biblical idiom for marital relations (Gen 4:1). "Until" (ἕως οὗ) marks the time up to the birth without settling what follows — Matthew's concern is only that Joseph had no part in the conception. The chapter ends as it aimed: the legal father names the child, and so Jesus is son of David.14

Endnotes

  1. 1:1 — βίβλος γενέσεως. The phrase is not the ordinary way to head a genealogy; it is the LXX wording of Gen 2:4 and 5:1 (Hebrew sefer toledot). Davies & Allison read it as deliberately multivalent — heading the genealogy, the birth account, and arguably the whole book: "the book of the new genesis wrought by Jesus." France: the effect on a Jewish ear is like John's "in the beginning." So Matthew opens by reaching behind David and Abraham to creation itself. Greek lexical entry: γένεσις on Logeion.
  2. 1:1 — χριστός as title. Here still "the Anointed One" (Hebrew māšîaḥ), a royal title, not yet a surname. By 1:18 Matthew already uses it almost as a name. Hart's translation deliberately renders it "the Anointed" throughout to keep the title audible; worth doing in the ear at 1:1.
  3. 1:1 — David before Abraham. The non-chronological order is purposeful: "son of David" (the royal-messianic title that recurs on the lips of the blind, the Canaanite woman, and the crowds) is fronted as Matthew's most urgent claim; "son of Abraham" reaches behind it to Gen 22:18 — "in your seed all the nations will be blessed" — opening the Gentile horizon the women and 28:19 will fill.
  4. 1:3–6 — the four women. Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, "the wife of Uriah" — not the matriarchs one expects. Three readings, best held together: (a) all are Gentiles or reckoned so — the Messiah's line runs through the nations (so Luz; cf. Ps. Sol. 17, which wanted Gentiles purged); (b) all are touched by sexual irregularity — so Mary's apparent scandal stands in a long line of God working through it; (c) all took bold initiative at the margins. Each converges on Mary, the climactic fifth.
  5. 1:6 — "the [wife] of Uriah." Matthew withholds "Bathsheba" and names the murdered Hittite husband instead, keeping David's adultery and judicial murder in view at the very summit he has just twice crowned "the king." Grace, not sanitization, is the genealogy's logic.
  6. 1:7–8, 10 — Ἀσάφ / Ἀμώς. The strongest text reads the names of the psalmist Asaph and the prophet Amos where the kings Asa and Amon belong. Possibly orthographic; possibly a deliberate (or traditional) folding of psalmody and prophecy into the royal line. Davies & Allison treat it as a genuine reading rather than scribal error. The apparatus also marks ordinary spelling variants (Βόες/Βοός, Ἰωβήδ).
  7. 1:11–12 — μετοικεσία and the Jeconiah question. "Deportation" tolls three times; the exile is the genealogy's fulcrum. A dispensational tradition (cf. Constable) argues Jesus had to bypass Joseph's line because of the curse on Jeconiah (Jer 22:30). But Matthew routes the legal line straight through Jeconiah: the point is not escape but that Messiah comes through Israel's catastrophe and judgment — the true return from exile in person (so France, Wright).
  8. 1:16, 20 — the broken chain & Joseph the dreamer. The active ἐγέννησεν gives way to passive ἐγεννήθη; ἐξ ἧς is feminine — Mary alone. Joseph, son of a Jacob (v. 16), receives revelation κατ' ὄναρ (the first of five prologue dreams), descends to Egypt, and is marked by chastity — a deliberate rhyme with the patriarch Joseph of Genesis.
  9. 1:17 — fourteen / gematria. דוד (D-V-D) = 4+6+4 = 14; David is the 14th and central name, his name stamped numerically over the whole. A possible second layer: 6×7 generations, Jesus inaugurating the seventh seven (cf. the Apocalypse of Weeks, 1 Enoch 93). The omitted kings confirm the structure is composed, not transcribed.
  10. 1:18 — γένεσις again. The harder reading γένεσις (vs. the "easier" γέννησις, "birth") is original and is what binds 1:18 back to 1:1 — one continuous "genesis." Copyists who changed it were flattening the link.
  11. 1:19 — δίκαιος. Two construals: righteous and yet unwilling to shame her (mercy tempering law), or righteous precisely in refusing the public exposure the law permitted. The latter increasingly favored and fits Matthew, where righteousness "exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees" (5:20) by bending toward mercy. δειγματίσαι = expose to public disgrace.
  12. 1:21 — Jesus / σώσει / Psalm 130. Ἰησοῦς = Hebrew Yēšûaʿ / Yəhôšuaʿ, "YHWH saves"; "for he will save" glosses the name. The whole clause echoes Ps 130:8 (129:8 LXX). Against the Second Temple end-of-exile hope, "save his people from their sins" relocates messianic deliverance from political liberation to forgiveness — fulfilled at the cup "poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" (26:28). "His people" will widen to "all the nations" (28:19).
  13. 1:23 — Isaiah 7:14 & Emmanuel. Original setting: a sign to Ahaz, c. 734 BC (Isa 7). Hebrew almah = young woman (not specifically betulah, virgin); the LXX rendered it παρθένος two-plus centuries before any Christian motive — so Matthew quotes a Jewish Greek text, not a fresh "mistranslation." He shifts "you/she will call" to "they will call," and treats Emmanuel as title, not the literal name given (Joseph names him Jesus). The translated name μεθ' ἡμῶν ὁ θεός frames the gospel: 1:23 → 18:20 → 28:20. NETS Isaiah (LXX).
  14. 1:25 — "until" & the variant. ἕως οὗ ("until") marks the period up to the birth as without relations, without itself settling the later period — so the verse cannot decide the perpetual-virginity question either way; Matthew's interest is solely the virginal conception. The longer reading "her firstborn son" is a harmonizing import from Luke 2:7; the short SBL reading "a son" is original.
