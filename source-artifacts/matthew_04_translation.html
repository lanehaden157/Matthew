The seam this unit sits on · 4:17

Kingsbury’s two “From that time Jesus began…” hinges (Ἀπὸ τότε ἤρξατο ὁ Ἰησοῦς) turn the whole book. Unit 4 straddles the first one: 4:12–16 closes the Introduction; 4:17 opens the body — and in the Bible Project design the two halves deliberately overlap at this verse.

I1:1–4:16
The Person of the Messiah. Origin, identity, authentication — before a public word. — our vv. 12–16 are its last beat
HINGE4:17
From that time Jesus began to proclaim: “Repent, for the kingdom of the skies has drawn near.” — belongs to both halves at once
II4:17–16:20
The Kingdom Proclaimed in Galilee. Word (chs. 5–7) and deed (chs. 8–9) — our vv. 17–25 are its overture. — matched by the 2nd hinge at 16:21 (“…began to show he must suffer”)

What Matthew did with Isaiah 9:1–2 · vv.15–16

A free citation matching neither the Hebrew nor the Greek cleanly: Matthew strips Isaiah 9:1 down to bare place-names, then quotes 9:2 more fully — with one telling change.

12Now, having heard that Iōannēs was handed over,1 he withdrew into the Galilee.

“Handed over,” παρεδόθη (paredothē) — the gospel’s passion-verb, used later of Judas and of Jesus himself. The forerunner’s fate prefigures the Son’s; and the king’s response is not confrontation but withdrawal.

13And leaving Nazara behind, having come, he settled in Kapharnaoum-beside-the-sea, in the territories of Zaboulōn and Nephthalim —

The geography is chosen for the proof-text to come: “beside-the-sea” (παραθαλασσίαν) in Zebulun and Naphtali — precisely Isaiah’s two tribes.

14so that what was spoken through Ēsaias the prophet might be fulfilled,2 saying:

15“Land of Zaboulōn and land of Nephthalim, way of the sea, beyond the Jordanēs, Galilee of the nations —

16the people, the one sitting in darkness, saw a great light,10 and to the ones sitting in a region and shadow of death — a light dawned upon them.”

Greekὁ λαὸς ὁ καθήμενος ἐν σκοτίᾳ φῶς εἶδεν μέγα NASB“the people who were sitting in darkness saw a great light” Text-formMatthew’s “sitting” diverges from both the Hebrew and the LXX, which have the people walking — a deliberate-looking change toward settled hopelessness (see note 2).

17From then Jesus began to proclaim and to say: “Repent,4 for the kingdom of the skies has drawn near.”3

Word-for-word identical to John’s opening at 3:2. One message, one kingdom; Jesus takes up the forerunner’s words exactly. Greekμετανοεῖτε, ἤγγικεν γὰρ ἡ βασιλεία τῶν οὐρανῶν NASB“Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.” Hartrenders μετανοεῖτε not as “repent” but as a turning or change of the whole mind/heart (meta + nous), shedding the penitential freight English “repent” has gathered. Cruxἤγγικεν is perfect tense — arrived-with-results or merely approached? The whole kingdom debate turns here (see note 3).

18Now, walking beside the sea of the Galilee, he saw two brothers — Simōn the one called Petros, and Andreas his brother — casting a cast-net6 into the sea; for they were fishers.

“He saw” (εἶδεν) — the same verb as v.16: the people saw the light; now the light does its own seeing and picks out working men (see note 10).

19And he says to them: “Come — after me — and I will make y’all fishers of men.”5

20And they, immediately leaving the nets, followed him.8

21And going on from there, he saw two other brothers — Iakōbos the son of Zebedaios, and Iōannēs his brother — in the boat with Zebedaios their father, mending their nets; and he called them.

22And they, immediately leaving the boat and their father, followed him.

The cost climbs across the two panels: the first pair leave the nets; the second leave the boat and their father — named, sitting right there. Discipleship as a widening leaving (note 8).

23And he went around in the whole of the Galilee, teaching in their synagogues9 and proclaiming the good-news of the kingdom, and healing every disease and every sickness among the people.

A thesis-statement for the whole Galilean ministry, repeated almost verbatim at 9:35. Teaching → sampled in chs. 5–7; healing → sampled in chs. 8–9; the heralding holds them together.

24And the report of him went out into the whole of Syria; and they brought to him all those having it badly with various diseases and torments — demon-oppressed and moon-struck9 and paralytic — and he healed them.

25And great crowds followed him — from the Galilee and Decapolis and Hierosoluma and Judea and beyond the Jordanēs.

The crowds “follow” too — the same verb as the four disciples (vv.20, 22). Is crowd-following disciple-following? The next verse (5:1) goes up the mountain, and the Sermon begins by sorting exactly that.

The pull from every direction · v.25

The great light dawns, and out of the shadow the crowds stream in from all sides — including Gentile Decapolis and the far side of the Jordan.

Notes

  1. ἀνεχώρησεν / παρεδόθη (v.12). “Withdrew,” ἀναχωρέω (anachōreō), is a Matthean signature — used far more than by the other evangelists and almost always for a strategic retreat from hostile power, not flight from fear. We first met it in Unit 2 (the holy family withdrawing to Egypt, then Galilee, 2:14, 2:22); it recurs at 12:15 and 14:13. The king Matthew paints does not seize ground; he withdraws and lets the light rise where it will. “Handed over,” παρεδόθη (paredothē), is the great passion-verb (26:15–16; 27:2; “the Son of Man handed over to sinners,” 26:45): John’s fate foreshadows Jesus’. ↗ lexis
  2. The Isaiah 9 citation (vv.15–16). The seventh and climactic fulfillment quotation of the Introduction (πληρόω, “fulfill,” the same formula as 1:22; 2:15, 17, 23). The text-form matches neither the Masoretic Hebrew nor the LXX cleanly: Matthew drops the connective grammar of Isaiah 9:1, keeping only the place-names (France notes he keeps exactly what makes the geography fit Capernaum), then quotes 9:2 more fully. Two deliberate touches: he changes “walking” to sitting in darkness (the posture of the settled and hopeless), and he keeps the haunting “shadow of death” = Hebrew צַלְמָוֶת (tsalmavet, cf. Ps 23). And “Galilee of the nations” (τῶν ἐθνῶν) quietly germinates the gospel’s last word, “all the nations” (28:19): the light dawns first in half-Gentile Galilee and ends up sent to every nation. ↗ Isa 9
  3. ἤγγικεν ἡ βασιλεία τῶν οὐρανῶν (v.17). The kingdom crux, first posed in Jesus’ own mouth. ἤγγικεν is perfect tense — arrived-and-pressing-in, or merely approached? Both are grammatically open, and here the great divide opens. Constable (dispensational) reads it as a genuine offer of the literal Davidic-millennial kingdom to national Israel, to be refused and so postponed to a future millennium — “at hand” meaning available-if-Israel-repents. France and Wright read inaugurated eschatology: God’s reign genuinely breaking into history in Jesus’ person, already-but-not-yet. The Bible Project frames it as sky’s rule coming down to reclaim the earth — which is why Matthew’s “kingdom of the skies” (vs. Mark/Luke’s “of God”) matters: not only reverent circumlocution for the divine name, but a pull toward “your kingdom come… on earth as in sky” (6:10). Held open by design.
  4. μετανοεῖτε (v.17). Present imperative plural: “y’all, keep changing.” The root is meta + nous — a turning of the whole mind-set, not merely sorrow for sins; Hart renders it accordingly as a change of heart/mind. We tracked the verb in Unit 3 on John’s lips (3:2); Jesus now takes it up verbatim. ↗ lexis
  5. ἁλιεῖς ἀνθρώπων, “fishers of men” (v.19). The unit’s richest tension. The warm evangelistic reading is real (Pseudo-Chrysostom already glosses it so), but the Old Testament background of fishing-for-people is overwhelmingly judgment: God’s “fishers” drag sinful Israel out for exile (Jer 16:16); hook and dragnet haul the nations (Hab 1:14–17); fishhooks take the complacent (Amos 4:2); Pharaoh is hooked like a sea-monster (Ezek 29:3–5). France: from the fish’s point of view, being caught is no blessing — and Matthew reuses net-imagery for the sorting dragnet of 13:47–50. So the best reading holds the tension: a mission gathering people for the moment of decision, which cuts both ways. Yet Jeremiah 16 sits in a chapter promising a return-from-exile greater than the exodus (16:14–15); if Jesus ends Israel’s long exile (cf. the genealogy, Unit 1), the fishers may gather scattered Israel home. Both notes ring at once. Note: ἀνθρώπων is the generic “humans,” so “of people” is defensible; NASB keeps the traditional “of men.”
  6. ἀμφίβληστρον (v.18). A New Testament hapax — the circular cast-net flung by a single fisher from shallows or boat, distinct from the σαγήνη dragnet of 13:47. The specificity reads like an on-the-ground Galilean memory.
  7. εὐθέως / ἀφέντες (vv.20, 22). “Immediately” marks the totality of the break — the same instantaneousness as Elisha’s in the call type-scene (1 Kgs 19:19–21, mantle over the plow), with Abraham’s call behind both (Gen 12: divine initiative, a promise to make the called one into something new, immediate obedience). “Leaving,” ἀφέντες, escalates: nets → boat-and-father. The leaving begun here at the nets runs forward to 10:37 (“whoever loves father or mother more than me…”) and 19:27 (“we have left everything”). Note too the rabbinic inversion: normally the disciple chose the rabbi; here the rabbi walks up and chooses working men, and calls them to follow not the Torah but him.
  8. The summary (vv.23–24). The triad teaching / proclaiming / healing programs the whole ministry and is repeated at 9:35 to frame the deed-block of chs. 8–9 — teaching sampled in chs. 5–7, healing in chs. 8–9. εὐαγγέλιον (“good-news”) is Matthew’s first of only four uses (9:35; 24:14; 26:13). “In their synagogues” carries a hairline distancing edge — the synagogue felt as “theirs” over against the community — a faint crack that becomes a structural fault-line later. “Moon-struck,” σεληνιαζομένους (selēniazomenous), is literally lunar-affected, usually understood as epilepsy. The doubled “every… every” is comprehensive-of-scope rather than a literal claim; healing the “incurable” is itself a messianic sign (Isa 35:5–6).
  9. εἶδεν, “saw” (vv.16, 18, 21). The seeing runs both ways: the people in darkness saw a great light (v.16); then the light, walking by the sea, saw his first followers (vv.18, 21) and called them. And in 5:14 he will hand the light back: “y’all are the light of the world.”
