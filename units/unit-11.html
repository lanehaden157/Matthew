The confusion of the forerunner · 11:2–6

2Now John, having heard in the jailhouse of the deeds of the Anointed One, having sent through his disciples,1

3said to him, "Are you the one who is coming, or should we keep expecting a different one?"2

4And answering, Jesus said to them, "Having gone, report to John what y'all hear and see:

5blind people see again and lame people walk around, lepers are made clean and the deaf hear, and dead people are raised, and poor people are told good news.3

Six items, and every one answers something already narrated in Units 5–9 — word and deed handed back to John as evidence, not argument.

6And blessed is whoever is not tripped up by me."4

The little beatitude — a gentle warning, not yet an accusation that John has already fallen.

The commendation of the forerunner · 11:7–15

7Now as these were going away, Jesus began to say to the crowds concerning John, "What did y'all go out into the wilderness to gaze at? A reed shaken by wind?5

8But what did y'all go out to see? A man dressed in soft things? Look — those who wear soft things are in kings' houses.

9But what did y'all go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell y'all, and more than a prophet.

10This is the one about whom it stands written: 'Look, I send out my messenger before your face, who will clear your road ahead of you.'"6

Greek (translit.)idou egō apostellō ton angelon mou pro prosōpou sou
NASB"Behold, I send my messenger before Your face"
Text-formA fusion of Exodus 23:20 (LXX wording matched closely) and Malachi 3:1 — with Malachi's "before me" recast as "before your face," turning a passage about Yahweh's own coming into one where Yahweh speaks to the Son about the Son's forerunner

11Truly I tell y'all, among those born of women there has not arisen anyone greater than John the Baptizer; but the smaller-one in the kingdom of the skies is greater than he.7

12And from the days of John the Baptizer until now, the kingdom of the skies is under assault, and forceful people seize it.8

The hardest crux in the unit — held open below, not resolved.

13For all the prophets and the law prophesied until John;

14and if y'all are willing to receive it, this one is Elijah, the one who is about to come.

15The one who has ears, let him hear.

Luke 16:16 — the same saying, differently placed and phrased

Matthew 11:12the kingdom of the skies suffers assault, and forceful people seize it
Luke 16:16the kingdom of God is announced, and everyone is forcing their way into it

Luke's version reads as unambiguously positive — everyone straining toward the kingdom — where Matthew's verb is genuinely double-edged and sits beside John's own imprisonment. Matthew keeps the ambiguity Luke resolves, which is one more reason not to force a single reading here.

Woe on the unrepentant towns · 11:20–24

20Then he began to reproach the towns in which most of his miracles had happened, because they did not change their minds.

21"Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! Because if the miracles that happened in y'all had happened in Tyre and Sidon, they would have changed their minds long ago, in sackcloth and ashes.

22But I tell y'all, it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon on the day of judgment than for y'all.

23And you, Capernaum — will you be lifted up to the sky? You will go down to Hades.11 Because if the miracles that happened in you had happened in Sodom, it would have remained until today.

24But I tell y'all that it will be more bearable for the land of Sodom on the day of judgment, than for you."

Thanksgiving and mutual knowledge · 11:25–27

25At that season, answering, Jesus said, "I openly acknowledge you, Father, Lord of the sky and the earth, because you hid these things from wise and perceptive people, and revealed them to infants.12

26Yes, Father, because thus it was well-pleasing before you.

27All things were handed over to me by my Father, and no one fully knows the Son except the Father, nor does anyone fully know the Father except the Son and whomever the Son wishes to reveal him to."13

The one bright use of paradidōmi in the whole gospel — every other occurrence is betrayal, arrest, or condemnation.

The invitation to the yoke · 11:28–30

28Come here to me, all who are laboring and having been burdened, and I will give y'all rest.14

Greek (translit.)deute pros me pantes hoi kopiōntes kai pephortismenoi
NASB"Come to Me, all who are weary and heavy-laden"
Text-form"Rest for your souls" (v. 29) follows the Hebrew of Jeremiah 6:16 (margôaʿ, "a resting-place") rather than the LXX's own rendering there, which leans toward "purification"

29Take my yoke upon y'all and learn from me, because I am gentle and lowly in heart, and y'all will find rest for your souls.15

30For my yoke does you good, and my burden is light."16

Notes

  1. christou (v. 2). The only place in Matthew where "Christ" stands entirely alone, with no "Jesus" attached — a name-title the narrator already assumes, while the man asking the question does not.
  2. ho erchomenos (v. 3). "The coming one" — John's own title for the Messiah from 3:11, now returned as a question. Cf. Psalm 118:26; Psalm 40:7; Isaiah 59:20. ↗
  3. the sixfold answer (vv. 4–5). Draws on Isaiah 35:5–6; 61:1; 29:18; 26:19. Isaiah 61:1 continues with "release to the captives," which Jesus' list omits — an argument from silence, held lightly, spoken to a man in prison. A comparable cluster (healing, resurrection, good news to the poor, together) appears in the Qumran text known as 4Q521.
  4. skandalisthē (v. 6). From skandalon, the trigger-stick of a trap. Opens here as a formally tracked thread; pays off catastrophically at 26:31–33, where every disciple is tripped up over Jesus in a single night.
  5. kalamon hypo anemou saleuomenon (v. 7). Herod Antipas's earliest coinage, minted at the founding of Tiberias, very probably bore a reed on the reverse. Whether Jesus is taking a deniable jab at the man now holding John is a live possibility, not a certainty.
  6. the conflated citation (v. 10). See compare box above. ↗
  7. ho mikroteros (v. 11). Three readings circulate: (1) Constable — status inside the kingdom outranks mere anticipation of it; (2) Chrysostom — Jesus names himself, "lesser" by age and by the crowd's own low estimate of him; (3) France — an era-marker, not a ranking of individuals. Held open.
  8. biazetai (v. 12). Middle or passive — grammatically ambiguous. Middle: the kingdom forcefully advances. Passive: the kingdom is being assaulted. Constable reads it as hostile opposition (the religious establishment seizing the kingdom on its own terms); Chrysostom reads it as the eagerness of true faith. Held open — see also the Luke 16:16 comparison above.
  9. the children's game (vv. 16–17). Piping/dancing (wedding) then dirge/mourning (funeral) in the complaint; John (mourning figure) then the Son of Man (feasting figure) in the explanation — a modest, reversed correspondence, not a load-bearing chiasm.
  10. ergōn vs. teknōn (v. 19). SBLGNT reads ergōn, "deeds" (the harder reading, and the one that completes the inclusio with v. 2); a number of witnesses, and Luke 7:35, read teknōn, "children."
  11. heōs ouranou hypsōthēsē… heōs hadou katabēsē (v. 23). Echoes Isaiah 14:13–15, the taunt-song against the king of Babylon — casting Capernaum, Jesus' own base, in Babylon's role.
  12. apo sophōn kai synetōn (v. 25). Echoes Isaiah 29:14 closely; the same chapter supplies v. 5's "the deaf will hear" (29:18) and will supply the Isaiah citation again at 15:8–9.
  13. panta moi paredothē (v. 27). See threads.payoffs note above. Dale Allison reads vv. 27–29 against Exodus 33:12–14 (Moses and God's mutual knowing, followed by a promise of rest) and Numbers 12:3 (Moses as praus, "gentle," LXX) — a Moses-typological layer alongside the Wisdom-Christology reading of v. 19 and vv. 28–30.
  14. rest for your souls (v. 28–29). See compare box above; Jeremiah 6:16 continues "but they said, we will not walk in it" — the refusal just enacted in vv. 20–24.
  15. the Sirach parallel (vv. 28–30). Sirach 51:23–27: come, take the yoke, learn/receive instruction, find rest, light labor — five points of contact in the same sequence. The difference is standing: Ben Sira points to Wisdom as her student; Jesus issues the invitation in his own person.
  16. chrēstos (v. 30). Root sense from chraomai, "to use" — "useful, serviceable, good for its purpose," the word the LXX uses to render Hebrew tov ("good") of God himself across the Psalms. Neither "easy" (Tyndale, 1525, the traditional English rendering) nor "well-fitting" (an invented gloss with no lexicographical support) is attested; "does you good" keeps the claim as one of benefit, not effort or fit.
