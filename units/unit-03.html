The testing — a threefold ordeal · 4:1–11

Each round runs setting → "if you are the Son of God" → Jesus' gegraptai ("it stands written"), all three answers drawn from one stretch of Deuteronomy, marching backward toward the Shema.

Panel A · The Forerunner and the Beloved Son — 3:1–17

1Now in those days there comes-on-the-scene1 John the Immerser, heralding in the wilderness of Judea

2and saying, "Turn! — for the kingdom of the skies has drawn near."

Greek Metanoeite, ēngiken gar hē basileia tōn ouranōn NASB "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand." Hart renders metanoeite "change your hearts" — turning, not mere remorse. Force ēngiken is perfect: "has drawn near / has arrived-and-stands." "At hand" understates it.2

3For this is the one spoken of through Isaiah the prophet, saying: "A voice of one crying-out in the wilderness — prepare the way3 of the Lord, make straight his paths." (Isa 40:3)

4And John himself had his clothing from camel's hair and a leather belt around his waist, and his food was locusts and wild honey.4

The Elijah costume (2 Kgs 1:8). Matthew is dressing John as the returning Elijah of Malachi 4:5 — made explicit later at 11:14; 17:12–13.

5Then there went out to him Jerusalem and all Judea and all the surrounding-country of the Jordan,

6and they were being immersed in the Jordan river by him, confessing-out their sins.5

7But seeing many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to his immersion, he said to them, "Offspring of vipers!6 Who warned y'all to flee from the coming wrath?

8So then, produce fruit worthy of the turning.

9And do not presume to say among yourselves, 'We have Abraham as father.' For I tell y'all that God is able from these stones to raise up children for Abraham.

A pun lost in Greek: in Hebrew/Aramaic God can raise banim (children) from ʾavanim (stones). Note "these stones" — the slanderer will reach for the same stones at 4:3.

10And already the axe lies at the root of the trees; so every tree not producing good fruit is cut down and thrown into fire.

11I myself immerse y'all in water unto turning, but the one coming after me is mightier than I — whose sandals I am not fit to carry. He himself will immerse y'all in Holy Spirit and fire,7

12whose winnowing-fork is in his hand, and he will thoroughly-clean his threshing-floor and will gather his wheat into the storehouse, but the chaff he will burn-down with unquenchable fire."

13Then there comes-on-the-scene Jesus from Galilee to the Jordan, to John, to be immersed by him.

The same verb (paraginetai) that introduced John in v.1 now introduces Jesus — herald and king arriving on the same stage.

14But John was-trying-to-prevent him, saying, "I have need to be immersed by you, and you come to me?"

15But answering, Jesus said to him, "Permit it now, for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness." Then he permits him.8

Greek houtōs gar prepon estin hēmin plērōsai pasan dikaiosynēn NASB "for in this way it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness." Reading Constable: Jesus' ethical conformity + solidarity with the godly remnant. France/Mackie: solidarity with sinful Israel — "walking through the waters of death with us." The "us" makes it jointly accomplished.

16And having been immersed, Jesus immediately went up from the water — and look, the skies were opened, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove9 and coming upon him.

17And look, a voice out of the skies, saying, "This is my Son, the beloved, in whom I delighted."10

Greek Houtos estin ho huios mou ho agapētos, en hō eudokēsa NASB "This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well-pleased." Three texts "my Son" = Ps 2:7 (king); "I delighted" = Isa 42:1 (Servant); "the beloved" = Gen 22:2 (Isaac, the son marked for offering). Vs Mark/Luke Matthew alone: "This is" (public) where they have "You are" (private to Jesus).
Panel B · The Testing of the Son — 4:1–11

1Then Jesus was led up into the wilderness by the Spirit, to be tested by the slanderer.11

The Spirit that descended (3:16) now drives. The purpose clause is startling: led there in order to be tested. The wilderness inclusio with 3:1 closes.

2And having fasted forty days and forty nights, afterward he hungered.

Forty: Israel's forty years compressed (Deut 8:2), and the fasts of Moses (Exod 34:28) and Elijah (1 Kgs 19:8). Chrysostom: he fasted no longer than they, lest the reality of his flesh be doubted.

3And coming, the tester said to him, "If you are Son of God, speak so that these stones may become loaves."

4But he, answering, said, "It stands written: 'Not on bread alone shall the human live, but on every word coming out through the mouth of God.'" (Deut 8:3)12

Greek ouk epʼ artō monō zēsetai ho anthrōpos, allʼ epi panti rhēmati ekporeuomenō dia stomatos theou NASB "Man shall not live on bread alone, but on every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God." Text-form Follows LXX Deut 8:3 ("every word coming out through the mouth of God"); MT: "all that proceeds from the mouth of the LORD."

5Then the slanderer takes him along into the holy city, and he stood him on the pinnacle (little-wing) of the temple,

6and says to him, "If you are Son of God, throw yourself down; for it stands written: 'He will command his angels concerning you, and upon their hands they will lift you, lest you strike your foot against a stone.'" (Ps 91:11–12)

The slanderer quotes Scripture too — and rightly, as to the words. The deviousness is the use: manufacture a crisis to force the Father's protective love, making the Son the master and God the servant.

7Jesus said to him, "Again it stands written: 'You shall not test-out the Lord your God.'" (Deut 6:16)

Deut 6:16's setting is Massah (Exod 17): Israel demanded God prove his presence by producing water. To trust God and to test God are opposites. Jesus will not repeat Massah.

8Again the slanderer takes him along to a very high mountain, and shows him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory,

9and said to him, "All these things I will give you, if falling-down you will worship me."13

10Then Jesus says to him, "Go away, Adversary! For it stands written: 'The Lord your God you shall worship, and him only shall you serve.'" (Deut 6:13)

Greek Kyrion ton theon sou proskynēseis kai autō monō latreuseis NASB "You shall worship the Lord your God, and serve Him only." Text-form LXX Deut 6:13 reads phobēthēsē ("you shall fear"); Matthew has proskynēseis ("you shall worship") — mirroring the devil's demand — and adds monō ("only"), which slams the door on divided loyalty.14 "Go away" Hypage, Satana — the same word Jesus throws at Peter in 16:23.

11Then the slanderer leaves him, and look, angels came and were ministering to him.15

The angels the devil tried to conscript (4:6) now come freely, on the Father's timing. diēkonoun ("were ministering") is the verb of 20:28 — the Son who came "to minister, not to be ministered to."

Notes

  1. "comes-on-the-scene" (v.1). paraginetai, historic present — vivid, "arrives." Matthew reuses it of Jesus at v.13, quietly pairing forerunner and king. "In those days" is a Septuagintal time-formula that leaps the ~30-year gap. ↗ Logeion
  2. "has drawn near" (v.2). ēngiken, perfect of engizō — a state achieved and standing. Constable hears a literal Davidic kingdom offered to Israel and later postponed; France and Wright hear God's reign actively breaking in. Matthew's basileia tōn ouranōn ("of the skies," plural Semitism for shamayim) is his signature for Mark/Luke's "kingdom of God" — reverent circumlocution plus the Danielic sky-to-earth reign (Dan 7). Not a place souls go after death.
  3. Isaiah 40:3 (v.3). The MT reads "a voice cries: in the wilderness prepare the way"; the LXX (which Matthew follows) reads "a voice crying in the wilderness" — locating the crier in the desert, exactly where John stands. The Qumran community used the same verse and reading to justify their desert settlement: 1QS 8:13–14, "to walk to the desert… as it is written, 'In the desert, prepare the way.'" ↗ DSS archive. hodos ("way") is a low-key motif here, not color-tracked.
  4. Elijah's garb (v.4). Camel hair + leather belt = 2 Kgs 1:8, the Tishbite. With Mal 3:1 (the messenger) and 4:5 (Elijah before the Day of the LORD), Matthew casts John as Elijah-returned. Locusts and wild honey: ritually clean desert fare (cf. Lev 11:22).
  5. "immersed… confessing-out" (v.6). baptizō = dip, immerse; LXX uses it of Naaman dipping in this same Jordan (2 Kgs 5:14). Ritual immersion was familiar (Lev 12–15; the explosion of mikvaot; the Essenes' daily washings, Josephus, War 2.129). John's novelty: a once-for-all, prophet-administered immersion tied to the nation's moral turning. exomologoumenoi = "confessing out," openly.
  6. "Offspring of vipers" (v.7). gennēmata echidnōn — and gennēmata shares the begetting root that opened the gospel: a pedigree, but a serpentine one (cf. Gen 3:15). Jesus reuses the very phrase against the same opponents at 12:34 and 23:33. The "coming wrath" (hē mellousa orgē) is the eschatological Day of the LORD. On Abraham's "merit" (v.9), the Second Temple confidence in descent from the patriarch — John demolishes it.
  7. "Holy Spirit and fire" (v.11). One immersion, two outcomes, unpacked by the threshing-floor of v.12: purifying refinement for the wheat (Mal 3:2–3, the refiner's fire), consuming judgment for the chaff. Removing a master's sandals was slave-work; John is not worthy even of that. Pentecost (Acts 2) heard the promise primarily as the gift of the Spirit.
  8. "fulfill all righteousness" (v.15). Jesus' first words in the gospel, and programmatic. plēroō is the formula-quotation "fulfill" root (Unit 2); dikaiosynē debuts here and will drive the Sermon (5:6, 20; 6:33). The plural "for us" makes righteousness jointly enacted — God's saving purpose carried out — not merely Jesus' private virtue. The sinless Son joins the penitent nation in the river, prefiguring how he will join them in death. ↗ Chrysostom Hom. 12
  9. The dove (v.16). Grammar leaves open whether "like a dove" modifies the Spirit or the manner of descent. The "dove = Holy Spirit" symbol is a later Christian development, not yet in first-century Jewish texts (Somov 2018). The strongest background is Gen 1:2, the Spirit "brooding" over the waters — glossed in b. Hagigah 15a as "like a dove which hovers over her young" (also Gen. Rab. 2:4). So: a new-creation signal — the Genesis Spirit broods over the water again, over the one risen from the Jordan. Secondary: Noah's dove over the flood-waters (Gen 8). ↗ Gen 1:2
  10. The voice (v.17). A fusion of three texts: ho huios mou = Ps 2:7 (Davidic king); en hō eudokēsa = Isa 42:1 (Servant — sealed by the Spirit just given; quoted in full at 12:18); ho agapētos = the LXX word for Isaac three times in Gen 22 (the beloved son marked for offering). King, Servant, and sacrificed beloved Son in one sentence. The Father's delight is pronounced before any ministry — sonship given, not earned.
  11. "tested by the slanderer" (v.1). peirazō spans "tempt" (toward failure) and "test/prove" (toward displayed character) — from metallurgy, to stress-test for true nature. Both God (Gen 22; Exod 20:20) and the accuser (Job 1–2) "test." diabolos = "slanderer," Greek for Hebrew śāṭān ("accuser"), the divine-council courtier (Job 1–2; 1 Kgs 22), here the personal Adversary named at v.10. The Spirit-led tour to temple and mountain reads as an apocalyptic vision-journey (cf. Ezek 8:3). The same root returns at the Lord's Prayer (6:13) and Gethsemane (26:41).
  12. Deut 8:3 (v.4) and the order of the citations. Jesus answers from one stretch of Moses' wilderness retrospective — 8:3, then 6:16, then 6:13 — walking backward through Deut 6–8 toward the Shema's heart. Deut 8:3's own context: God let Israel hunger and fed them manna to teach trust in his word. Israel failed (Exod 16; Num 11); the true Son passes — his hunger is the Father's will, not to be short-circuited.
  13. The mountain and the kingdoms (vv.8–9). The first of Matthew's three mountains (testing 4 / teaching 5 / commission 28). The devil offers "all the kingdoms… and their glory" if Jesus will proskyneō him — the magi's worship-word (Unit 2). On the mountain of 28:18 the risen Son receives "all authority" — the same universal dominion, but by the road of the cross, not the shortcut of worshiping the Adversary. Rev 11:15 ("the kingdom of the world has become the Lord's") and Phil 2:6–11 are the canonical answers.
  14. "worship… him only serve" (v.10). Matthew's proskynēseis ("worship") for the LXX's phobēthēsē ("fear") is a deliberate adjustment to mirror the devil's demand (Keener; Allison); the added monō ("only," attested in LXX/versions) makes the verse decisive against divided worship. latreuseis = cultic service. The line is drawn at the Shema: undivided worship of YHWH alone — the point where Israel (the golden calf) failed.
  15. The cross-echo and the forward arc (v.11). "If you are the Son of God" (4:3, 6) returns verbatim on the mockers' lips at 27:40 ("if you are the Son of God, come down") — the temptation's last round, refused again. Forward reception: Heb 2:18; 4:15 (tested as we are, yet without sin); 1 Cor 10:1–13 (Israel's wilderness as typoi of Christian testing); Rom 5 / 1 Cor 15 (the last Adam prevails where the first fell); James 1:13 (wrestling with the test/tempt ambiguity).
